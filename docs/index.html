Pipeline / current core + future branches

One sky, several routes.

The implemented system is one branch of a larger sky-localisation framework. Follow the routes that exist today, then the condition-dependent layers and research paths that are still being defined.

The visible sky decides which branch is credible.

Time of day, cloud cover, visibility, motion evidence, confidence, and detected object classes should become a selection gate. These are research hypotheses, not automatic decisions in the current implementation.

D / future observation layers

The roadmap is layered, not linear.

These branches are deliberately greyed out. They describe research directions from the roadmap, not capabilities the current dashboard should imply are complete.

11Stars / catalogue identityfuture

Make stellar points nameable

Stars are the most stable orientation reference, but the current detector still reports many raw points as unknown.

  • Pattern extraction match geometric arrangements against a catalogue.
  • Known directions attach HIP identifiers, angular uncertainty, and confidence.
  • Solver input feed identified stars into the existing ephemeris path.
12Satellites / orbital layerexploratory

Use predictable moving references

Satellites sit between inertial stars and atmospheric motion: fast-moving, but potentially predictable from orbital data.

  • Reject or identify separate moving points from stellar candidates.
  • Orbit + UTC predict directions for a candidate robot position.
  • Localization test additional absolute constraints without making them a dependency.
13Aircraft / intermediate layerexploratory

Track opportunistic moving landmarks

Aircraft are not static landmarks. Their motion mixes aircraft movement, robot motion, and camera rotation.

  • Detection / rejection separate moving aircraft from stable celestial candidates first.
  • Optional ADS-B associate position, altitude, heading, and timestamp when available.
  • Parallax compare aircraft depth with cloud and stellar layers.
14Clouds / atmospheric layersfuture

Read atmosphere as motion, not truth

High, mid, low, and vertically developing clouds have different persistence, deformation, and wind behaviour.

  • Perception segment, classify, and estimate layer altitude with uncertainty.
  • Tracking use optical flow and approximately 30 seconds of overlapping history.
  • Parallax compare motion across cloud layers and distant celestial references.
  • Role estimate relative odometry, not absolute global position by itself.

E / long-term estimator

Turn whichever layers exist into one state estimate.

The eventual architecture is a condition-adaptive estimator, not a larger single detector. It should separate absolute pose cues from relative motion cues and carry uncertainty forward.

15Rotation compensationfuture

Remove camera motion before sky motion

IMU gravity, roll, pitch, and rotational velocity could isolate residual sky motion before translation and atmospheric movement are estimated.

  • Observed motion split robot motion, atmosphere, and deformation.
  • IMU fusion compensate the largest rotational component first.
16Daylight orientationfuture

Add cues when stars are absent

Polarised sunlight could add Sun-direction and heading constraints when the solar disc is obscured. Horizon and terrain could provide local geometric context.

  • Polarisation complementary absolute orientation.
  • Horizon / terrain local constraint, not global celestial truth.
17Condition-adaptive fusionfuture

Choose reliable evidence in context

A probabilistic or factor-graph estimator could combine stars, Sun, Moon, satellites, aircraft, clouds, weather priors, IMU, and robot odometry without requiring every layer.

  • Offline mode camera + ephemerides + IMU.
  • Enhanced mode optional weather, ADS-B, and orbital information.
  • Output confidence-aware robot pose and relative motion.
